# PTO Policy (2022, archived)

This policy explains how paid time off works at Acme Example. It applies to every full-time and part-time employee from their first day. Paid time off covers vacation, personal days, and short absences that are not medical. Sick leave and parental leave are covered in separate policies. This version was replaced on 1 January 2025 and is kept only for the record.

## Accrual

Full-time employees receive 20 days of paid time off per calendar year. The days accrue monthly, so an employee who joins in July receives half of the annual amount. Part-time employees accrue in proportion to their scheduled hours. Accrued time is shown in the people portal on the first working day of each month.

## Requesting time off

Submit a request in the people portal at least five working days before the first day of leave. Your manager approves or declines the request within three working days. For absences of more than ten consecutive days, speak to your manager before you submit. Approval is not guaranteed in the last week of a quarter, when many teams close their books or ship releases, so plan long breaks early.

## Carry-over and payout

Up to 3 unused days carry over into the next year and expire on 31 March. Days above that limit are lost on 31 December. Acme Example does not pay out unused days during employment. When employment ends, the final paycheck includes accrued but unused days, as local law requires.

## Public holidays and closures

Public holidays do not count against paid time off. When the company closes between 24 December and 1 January, those days are also free of charge to your balance. If you work a public holiday because of on-call duty, you earn one day of time off in lieu, which you should use within 60 days.

## Sick days and short notice

Do not use paid time off for illness; report sick days to your manager on the first morning and record them in the people portal. If you fall ill during planned leave and bring a medical note, the sick days are returned to your balance. Unplanned personal emergencies, such as a burst pipe at home, may be booked as paid time off after the fact if you tell your manager as soon as you can. Managers should be flexible and avoid asking for details that are private.
